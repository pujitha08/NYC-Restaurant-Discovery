## Data quality issues and decisions needed

1. **Document length vs embedding limits.** A concatenated restaurant document has a median of 2,177 words; 89.7% of restaurants exceed 256 words and 80.5% exceed 512. One embedding per restaurant would truncate most of them. Decide: embed individual reviews (or chunks) and aggregate scores per restaurant, or select a subset of reviews per restaurant for the document.
2. **Short reviews.** 17.2% of reviews have fewer than 5 words and the median is 14 words. Decide whether to drop reviews below a minimum length for the index, or keep them for TF-IDF only.
3. **Duplicate review texts.** 99,714 redundant copies of reviews with 5+ words (3.6%). Decide whether to de-duplicate exact repeats before indexing, otherwise they inflate term frequencies and evidence lists.
4. **URLs, translations and non-English text.** 37 reviews contain URLs, 238,807 are "(Translated by Google)" blends, and at least 0.5% are mostly non-ASCII. Decide whether to strip URLs and the translation markers, and whether to filter or separately handle non-English reviews (English-only dietary regexes miss them). Translated blends carry the original foreign-language text after "(Original)", so they are partly non-English even though the ASCII heuristic misses them.
5. **Null rating / user_id.** 2,007 reviews have a null rating and 2,007 a null user_id (the same records). They are excluded from the rating and user statistics. Decide whether to drop them from the corpus.
6. **Prolific users.** The most prolific user wrote 955 in-scope reviews. Among the top 10, distinct restaurants are at least 96% of reviews and no user repeats the same text more than 5 times, so copy-paste spam is not evident; they look like heavy contributors. Decide whether to cap reviews per user per restaurant anyway, or leave users unweighted.
7. **MISC dietary attributes are almost absent.** Only vegetarian (5,289) and halal (485) attributes exist; none for vegan, kosher, gluten-free, dairy or allergens. Review text is therefore the primary evidence for most diets. Decide how much to trust the structured attributes versus mentions where they disagree (see the cross-check table).
8. **Sparse allergen evidence.** 633 restaurants (4.1%) are low_evidence, and nut/shellfish allergy mentions exist for about 1% of restaurants. Decide how the app should respond when there is no evidence (say so explicitly, never imply safety).
9. **Review-count cap and rating-only reviews.** 9 restaurants have num_of_reviews of 9,998 or more, which looks like a source cap. `num_of_reviews` counts rating-only reviews, so it overstates the text we have; use `num_text_reviews` for evidence-based decisions.
10. **Field coverage.** Price is present for 70.0% of restaurants and descriptions for 62.1%, so they can't be used as hard filters. 106 restaurants carry a won-denominated price (₩) instead of $, so price symbols are not comparable across the set. Decide whether price becomes a soft preference only and how to treat ₩ values.
11. **Borough labels are heuristic.** Boroughs come from ZIP code, then city name. A handful of records are known to be wrong (e.g. a Brooklyn address with a Manhattan ZIP, Liberty and Ellis Island cafes). Decide whether to add a latitude/longitude sanity check, or accept the error rate.
12. **Timestamps.** 287 reviews predate 2005, of which 254 are dated exactly 1990-12-31, which is a placeholder, not a real date. Real volume starts around 2016 and peaks in 2018-2019, and restaurant status (open/closed) is a 2021 snapshot. Decide how to treat the placeholder dates, and whether to weight recent reviews.
13. **Category scope.** Restaurants are matched on any category, so secondary tags such as Bar (1,637), Coffee shop (1,390), Bakery (708) and Caterer (516) are in scope. Decide whether to keep them or restrict to restaurants whose primary category is food service.
